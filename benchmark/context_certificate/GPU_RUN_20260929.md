## Offline replay results

Reports are in [the replay directory](artifacts/h200-20260929/replay/); each
capture replayed at 4/6/8 bits in both bound modes against the native
top-512 selections and native FP32 scores. All six reports pass: every
native selection survives screening at every precision (zero winners
excluded, zero native-score interval violations), and the maximum absolute
difference between native scores and the float64 reference on identical BF16
inputs is 2.0–2.7e-6. Selection order is not expected to match and does not.

Estimated index-read traffic versus one shared BF16 index scan per
verification (modeled bytes from packed storage, not measured GPU traffic):
4-bit screening keeps 68–100% of blocks and estimates 0.96–1.28× a full
scan, so the full-scan fallback triggers on five of six captures; 6-bit
joint screening keeps 17–35% of blocks at 0.58–0.68× (best per capture);
8-bit keeps 11–17% at 0.63–0.70×. Adjacent-position selection Jaccard
within a verification ranges 0.21–0.67, and the union of selected
microblocks across a verification's four positions is 759–1023 of roughly
8,080 finalized blocks.

These six verifications establish the capture hook's first successful GPU
operation on real model data and one layer's screening behavior; they do
not establish model-wide quality, long-context feasibility, 32-request
concurrency, or the 80-token/s target.

## Economics follow-up (2026-09-29, host offline)

The H200 went unreachable (~08:10 UTC) before the follow-up measurements,
with all captures already local. Two of the three remaining proof steps were
completed without a GPU, in [the proof kit](proof_kit/PROOF_PLAN.md):

- A torch port of the certified screen reproduces the NumPy probe exactly —
  picks and candidate unions at 4/6/8 bits, and picks equal to the native
  selections as sets — on all six captures ([proof_kit/torch_screen.py](proof_kit/torch_screen.py)).
- Replacing the S=4 budget's assumed 10% refinement fraction with the
  measured fractions moves screening's value to ~1 ms of a 37.5 ms pass
  (2.8%) on GPU bandwidth, while its proposed host-refinement placement
  nearly doubles host-link load (48 → 84–97 ms at 20 GB/s), the binding
  resource ([screening-economics.json](artifacts/h200-20260929/screening-economics.json)).
  As a throughput intervention the byte-level case for a production
  screening kernel is negative at S=4.

The two GPU measurements were then completed on a 2× RTX PRO 6000 spot
instance before it was reclaimed ([results](artifacts/rtx2x-20260929/)):
the QSA index stage measured **4.12%** of decode-window GPU kernel time
(0.79 ms per verify step; MoE GEMM 62.5%), and at target scale the index
stage is dominated by top-512 selection compute, not key-scan bandwidth,
with the unfused screened path 8.3× slower and zero certified-interval pick
mismatches. The screening kernel proposal is dropped by its own decision
rule; the open budget questions are expert-read traffic and the host link.

## De-risk measurements for selection-driven prefetch (2026-09-29, one RTX PRO 6000)

Both follow-up measurements ran on a single card
([summary](artifacts/rtx1-20260929/summary.json)):

- **Cross-layer overlap (layers 3→7, six verifications of the 32k request):
  coverage 0.65, Jaccard 0.48, overfetch 0.35.** Early-layer selections
  predict 65% of the adjacent layer's selections — above the 0.5 threshold
  that keeps selection-driven prefetch alive, with admission control
  expected to reclaim part of the 35% overfetch.
- **Batch scaling (N=1/4/8/16 distinct ~30k requests, 64+ decode steps
  profiled each): GEMM grows only 1.51× from N=1 to N=8** (11.9 → 18.0
  ms/step) where the budget's uniform-iid expert-union model predicts 5.9×;
  N=4 and N=8 are indistinguishable (expert amortization saturates early).
  At N=8 one card sustains ~121 tok/s per request (~968 tok/s aggregate) at
  ~30k contexts — the 80 tok/s per-request target is already met with
  headroom. N=16 (550k-token pool) measured 57.5 ms/step under mixed
  prefill/KV-contention confounds and needs a clean steady-state rerun.
  The index stage stays ~1 ms/step across all N (share 4.3% → 1.8%).

Consequences: the capacity model's expert-union assumption must be replaced
with measured route correlation before any 32×256k claim; prefetch value
must be re-derived against the measured (much better) batch scaling; the
aggregate 2,560 tok/s target's remaining gap lives in the large-batch
contention regime, not in indexing.
